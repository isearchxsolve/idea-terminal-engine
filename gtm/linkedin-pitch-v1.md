# LinkedIn pitch v1 — IDEA-TERMINAL-ENGINE as a service

## The post (copy-paste ready)

Most startup ideas don't die from bad ideas. They die from slow, expensive
validation.

You spend 6–18 months and serious capital building something — only to learn,
after the burn, that it couldn't have worked technically. Or that it could
have, and you hesitated too long.

I'm building a service that collapses that loop.

→ You give me your idea in plain language. Captured verbatim, timestamped,
untouched — the contract of what you asked for.
→ An evidence-bound autonomous engine distills it, blueprints it, builds it,
and tests it against its own requirements. No "the AI said it's done" — every
completion claim is backed by executable evidence.
→ In weeks, you get one of two outcomes. Both are wins:

1. A live, working prototype — your Go-To-Market v1 — that real users can
   touch, sign up for, or ignore.
2. A documented, evidence-backed proof the idea doesn't hold up technically —
   before you spend real capital on it.

To be clear about what this is NOT: we verify technical feasibility. Not
market demand. Anyone selling you "validated business" at this stage is
selling fiction. What you get instead is rarer: the truth, fast, cheap, and
with receipts.

Failure becomes a low-cost signal, not a multi-month loss.

First three beta clients run the full pipeline at cost. DM "TERMINAL" and
bring the idea you've been sitting on.

## Positioning notes (internal — do not post)

- Target audience: non-technical founders, indie hackers, solo inventors,
  small agencies that quote 6-figure MVPs. The pain is "I have an idea and
  a quote that costs more than my savings."
- The two-outcome framing IS the product. Never apologize for outcome 2 —
  it is the cheaper version of the client's own worst case, delivered early.
- Mandatory scope disclaimer in every reply and DM: "We verify technical
  feasibility and ship a live GTM v1. We do not and cannot guarantee market
  success. What happens after real users touch it is signal, not verdict."
  (Mirrors governance/stack-v2.md and the engine's RELEASE language.)
- Objection: "I can just ask ChatGPT to build it." Answer: asking a chatbot
  is a claim; this is a pipeline with receipts — state machine, budgets,
  independent verification, anti-triviality gates, audit log. The difference
  is evidence.
- Objection: "Why weeks, not days?" Answer: bounded repair loops and real
  browser E2E testing take compute time; anything faster is pretending.
- Pricing anchor for beta: at-cost = your inference + hosting spend, itemized
  per run from the run's own budget tracker. Transparency is the brand.
